A Field Guide
to Network Intelligence

Guide No. 1's networks, now with machine learning inside: predict the tie that forms next, embed a whole graph into geometry, let neural networks read structure — and keep the skepticism that Wasserman & Faust drilled into the field.
Orientation

Foundation

Nine ideas that separate "AI on networks" from alchemy — what the machine actually sees when it looks at an adjacency matrix, and what it systematically misses.

Computational Social Science

Society leaves digital traces; computation reads them at scale (Lazer et al., 2009). Networks are the native data structure of social life — CSS made them observable by the million.

ML for Networkers

Supervised vs unsupervised, training/test splits, overfitting. A link predictor is just a classifier on dyads — Guide No. 2's discipline applies node by node, pair by pair.

Graph Representation Learning

Map every node to a vector so that structural similarity becomes geometric proximity — DeepWalk and node2vec (random-walk embeddings) made networks consumable by any ML model.

Graph Neural Networks

Message passing: each node aggregates its neighbors' features, layer by layer — GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT. The network is no longer the input's shape; it is the computation.

Link Prediction

Score every unobserved dyad by topology — common neighbors, Adamic–Adar, preferential attachment, Katz — and rank (Liben-Nowell & Kleinberg, 2007). The oldest task in network AI; the lab below.

ML Community Detection

From modularity maximization (Louvain) to deep clustering: communities as latent variables the model infers, not just cuts in a diagram. Detection becomes estimation.

Networks from Text

Entity and relation extraction turn documents, hearings, and archives into edges before analysis begins. NLP builds the graph; SNA interrogates it. Garbage extraction, garbage graph.

Synthetic Networks & Simulation

ER and preferential-attachment generators, graph GANs, agent-based models: fabricate networks with known ground truth to test methods before trusting them on real data.

Ethics of Network AI

Relational data outs people twice — you, and everyone you connect to. Homophily makes predictions self-fulfilling: predict collaboration, target invites, manufacture the tie you predicted.

Research Programs

Six Traditions

From statistical physics to graph neural networks — the lineages that built network intelligence, and the measurement theorists who keep it honest.

The Network Scientists

Watts (1998) · Barabási & Albert (1999) · Barabási (2016)
Small worlds and scale-free growth: generative models where structure itself — degree distributions, hubs, clustering — is the phenomenon. Physics brought networks to the masses.
Use when asking how global structure arises from local rules.

The Predictors

Liben-Nowell & Kleinberg (2007) · Lichtenwalter et al. (2010)
Link prediction as a disciplined task: topology-based scores, temporal train/test splits, and honest metrics. Turned "who will collaborate next?" into falsifiable ranking.
Use when the claim is about the future of a network.

The Embedders

Perozzi et al. (2014, DeepWalk) · Grover & Leskovec (2016, node2vec)
Random walks as sentences, nodes as words: embeddings that preserve neighborhoods, roles, or equivalences depending on walk bias. The graph becomes a feature matrix; every downstream model gets to use it.
Use when you need numeric node features for ML.

The Neural School

Kipf & Welling (2017) · Hamilton et al. (2017) · Veličković et al. (2018)
Graph convolution, inductive sampling, attention over neighbors — GNNs learn both features and structure, and generalize to nodes the model has never seen. State of the art on prediction, classification, and detection.
Use when node attributes and structure jointly carry signal.

The Computational Social Scientists

Lazer et al. (2009, 2020) · Edelmann et al. (2020)
Society-scale digital data plus social theory — and a manifesto's worth of warnings about data drift, platform black boxes, and theory-free empiricism. The field that named the obstacles.
Use when claims are about human behavior at platform scale.

The Skeptics

Wasserman & Faust (1994) · Borgatti, Everett & Johnson (2013)
A predicted tie is not a real tie: measurement error, boundary specification, and the difference between structure and its shadow. The classical canon is the immune system network AI needs.
Use when model output is about to meet social theory.
Inferential Layer

The Model Path

Six steps from raw traces to defensible network AI. The cross-trail links point into No. 1 (Networks) and the methodology map — prediction without the classical canon is just curve-fitting on dyads.

Harvest Relational Data

Digital traces, meeting minutes, citations, APIs, and text: extract entities and relations with NLP, deduplicate, and only then draw the graph. Boundary specification (Guide No. 1) decides what the model is allowed to know.
Data: traces or documents Entry: low
Framed in the methodology map: NLP & Topic Modeling → methodology map.

Embed the Graph

node2vec-style random-walk embeddings or GNN hidden states turn each node into a vector. Similarity in embedding space ≈ structural equivalence — the bridge between graph theory and every sklearn-style pipeline you already know.
Data: one network, optionally node attributes Entry: medium
Framed in the methodology map: Machine Learning Prediction → methodology map.

Predict Links & Labels

Rank unobserved dyads (the lab below), or classify nodes by their embeddings. Split by time, not at random: predict 2026 from 2024, or you are scoring interpolation, not foresight. Report precision@k and calibration, not just AUC.
Data: network + honest split Entry: medium

Detect with ML

Modularity methods for speed, deep clustering when communities overlap or are latent. Validate partitions against theory-driven groupings — a community the algorithm finds but the theory cannot name is a finding, not a conclusion.
Data: network, theory of groups Entry: medium–high
Framed in the methodology map: Community Detection & Clustering → methodology map.

Simulate & Stress-Test

Generate synthetic networks with planted structure, run agent-based models, and ask whether your pipeline recovers what you planted. If the method cannot find a known community, it will happily invent an unknown one.
Data: a generative model Entry: medium
Framed in the methodology map: Agent-Based Modeling → methodology map.

Explain & Govern

Interpret which structural features drive predictions; audit bias and privacy exposure in relational data; resist causal language — an accurate predictor of collaboration is not evidence that collaboration is predictable in nature. For causal claims, cross to Guide No. 2.
Data: model + audit + theory Entry: frontier
Framed in the methodology map: Causal Machine Learning → methodology map.
The Workbench

AI at the Workbench

The sections above put machine learning on the network. This one puts it in your workflow — how AI assistants actually change the daily practice of SNA research, stage by stage, with the failure mode named next to each gift.

From Text to Edge List

Minutes, hearings, archives → candidate edges: LLMs extract actors and relations at document scale. But every extraction is a coding decision — audit a sample the way you would a human coder's work.

Name Deduplication

"City of Springfield / Springfield / SPRGFLD" → one node. Entity resolution across rosters and databases, in seconds. Verify merges by hand: merging two real actors is worse than duplicating one.

Code That Runs

Write, debug, and translate network code — statnet ↔ igraph ↔ networkx ↔ PyTorch Geometric. An infinitely patient pair programmer. House rule: execute everything it writes; never ship a line you haven't run.

The Adversarial Reviewer

Paste your research design (anonymized!) and ask the model to attack it: boundary problems, endogeneity, SUTVA violations. It is a sparring partner that never gets tired of finding your confounds — see Guide No. 2.

Canon Navigation

Map a debate's citation graph, summarize what the ERGM and SAOM literatures disagree about, find the bridge paper between two traditions. Caveat: LLMs fabricate references with total confidence — verify every citation at the source.

Teaching & Translation

Generate practice networks, explain centrality to a mayor or ERGM to a methods class, then let students interrogate the model — watching it fail teaches epistemic humility faster than any lecture.

When AI touches a social network, the network touches real people.
01 · Anonymize first
A respondent network is personal data about third parties — people who never consented to a cloud API.
02 · Run every line
Generated code is a draft, not a result. If it isn't executed and read, it doesn't exist.
03 · Verify every citation
Hallucinated references are fluent and confident. Check each one against the original.
04 · Confidence is decoration
The model's tone carries no evidence. Weight claims by verification, not eloquence.
Laboratory

The Prediction Lab

A collaboration network of twelve cities. Six pairs are on the verge of signing — three of them did, just after the observation window closed. Those three are hidden from you: they are the future. Pick a scoring metric, rank the ten candidate pairs, then reveal how many future ties your metric caught in its top three. This is link prediction, the way Liben-Nowell & Kleinberg framed it.

Supplies

Toolkit & Canon

What we install, and what we keep within arm's reach.

Software

  • Python: networkx / igraph / graph-tool for the graph itself; scikit-learn for classical ML; PyTorch Geometric or DGL for GNNs; Karate Club or gensim for embeddings; spaCy (or an LLM, carefully) for relation extraction.
  • R: igraph + tidygraph + ggraph for analysis and drawing; keep statnet nearby — statistical models are how you check what the black box found.
  • LLM assistants: excellent for boilerplate and data wrangling — and prolific hallucinators of APIs. Run every line; trust nothing unexecuted.
  • Benchmarks: SNAP (Stanford), KONECT, and Network Repository for standard graphs — karate club, dolphins, political blogs, citation networks.
